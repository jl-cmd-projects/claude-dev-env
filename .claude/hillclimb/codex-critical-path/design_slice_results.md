# Can a reviewer distinguish a settled design check from a missing prerequisite?

A planning review on October 5 exposed a scope decision. The operator wanted a broad design backed by prototypes, and asked why detailed retry testing still occupied the team. Already-completed work supported a concrete design choice. Later revisions also found a genuine issue: some validation inputs would be rejected before they exercised the behavior being claimed. The historical author used Claude Opus 5.5. The local cases reconstruct this decision for Codex; they use fictional workspaces and do not reproduce the historical author's actions.

The ledger task asks for an evidence-backed design, complete coverage and acceptance rows, and a generated review page. The two development cases differ only in one opening receipt. In one case it supports the delayed request claim. In the other the probe never reaches the pertinent branch; a new request-ID-qualified run is necessary. The reserve asks for a gallery-return design and coverage of a different producer path. The user goal stays fixed in all six workers.

## What the workers and reviewers did

Exactly six new workers started, one GPT-6.2 Sol High and one GPT-6.1 Sol High per case. Five reached a fork: two due to the corrected visible-only review trigger and three due to the six-command sampling boundary. The sixth completed its reserve task before the trigger. The ten paired continuations inherited their original conversations and matched checkout bytes. The platform receipts identify launched workers, with model and effort recorded from the launch arguments. No user intervened and every continuation finished within budget.

The two new GPT-6.2 Sol High reviewers both returned PASS at the checkpoint. Each saw the worker leave an incidental Git error and read the design evidence. No solver received a redirect. They consumed 9.39 and 7.96 active seconds. The trigger did not select either Sol 6.1 development worker, which had been running and saving prototype receipts. Both held-out workers proceeded without review. Timing differences between these baseline and candidate arms cannot be credited to advice.

All eight ledger outcomes recorded a delayed run with qualifying request IDs and produced pages that independently regenerated byte-for-byte. All four outcomes facing the missing evidence repaired it. All four whose opening evidence already reached the relevant branch also ran another delayed probe. These are outcome counts across forks; some recorded actions preceded the fork and were shared. The returned scenario and the command history let a grader distinguish different experiments from another run answering the same question. The gallery runs exercised the supplied captures, including delayed image completion.

Both readers counted four wholly irrelevant continuation calls. Three of the four were the same calls. They found 17 and 14 redundant subprobes respectively, or 16 and 13 after counting a shared pre-fork probe once. Those counts include unnecessary components inside otherwise useful shell calls. Neither reader found a sustained diversion. The main correctness miss was a design that allowed a second debit on the same expired key without a reconciliation prerequisite. The paired plan addressed it, but its reviewer returned PASS and no advice reached the worker. Chance variation between continuations remains a sufficient explanation. A user follow-up is a blind assessment of need; no user was asked to intervene during these trials.

**Why the strict oracle and human grades differ.** Independent replay regenerated all eight ledger pages, replayed all 31 saved probe receipts and eight opening receipts, and matched all six gallery capture rows. All 16 ledger coverage and acceptance CSVs had the requested rows and statuses. Literal checks rejected expressions such as “transport IDs” and “atomically,” including in otherwise correct plans. Four workers also added design-check scripts or model receipts outside the oracle's allowlist; no frozen runtime input changed. The gallery grader rejected alternative headings or ways of stating its deployment limitation, including a matched pair with byte-identical deliverables. All three plans limited the local traces to the tested desktop path. The original strict zero is preserved. Two blinded readers separately accepted 10 of 11 artifacts; one of those 10 still left a cleanup-timing concern open. The stronger judgment rests on the files and replay, not on the submission summaries.

**Decision.** This case elicited actual redundant investigations that the earlier desk case did not. The observed reviewer policy delivered zero redirects and leaves the causal improvement unproven. It opened early on two incidental errors; other workers saved probe outputs without triggering it. The held-out task supplies no reviewed exposure. The experiment produced no production PR, merge, release, installation or observed activation. Its safety and approval rules were unchanged.
